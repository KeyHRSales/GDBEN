Good to know

When coverage begins and ends

Begins: the 1st of the month following the company waiting period.

Ends: if you no longer meet eligibility requirements, contributions are discontinued, or Gator's Dockside is no longer associated with Key HR.

Once you make your elections, you cannot change them until the next open enrollment unless you have a qualifying event.

Qualifying events

You have 30 days to make a change

  • Change in employment status or legal marital status
  • Change in number of dependents
  • Unpaid leave of absence or FMLA leave
  • Entitlement to Medicare or Medicaid
  • A dependent gains or loses eligibility
  • A COBRA-qualifying event
  • A move where your current carrier is not available

RBP

Reference-Based Pricing

SBMA · Zelis

RBP plans pay doctors and hospitals based on the Medicare fee schedule plus a percentage, which lowers costs for you and your employer. You can choose any provider.

  1. Choose your provider. All providers are covered up to the plan's allowable amount.
  2. Schedule your visit and share your plan information. The provider can verify benefits using the number on your ID card.
  3. Need support? SBMA customer service connects you with a Zelis Member Advocate, who can also explain payment to your provider.
  4. Billing. Pay your copays, coinsurance and any deductible. If you get a balance bill, call SBMA and a dedicated advocate will work to lower or remove it.

Health Savings Account

Tax-advantaged, yours for life.

When paired with a qualifying High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP), an HSA gives you triple tax advantages and a balance that stays with you year after year. HSA limits follow the calendar year. Only available if you are enrolled in an HSA-qualified HDHP. See IRS Publication 502 for eligible expenses.

Why open an HSA?

Flexible options

Use funds tax-free for qualified medical, dental and vision care, now or in the future, even if your HSA-compatible coverage ends.

Tax advantages

Contributions are tax-deductible or pre-tax. Earnings grow tax-deferred. Qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

Long-term growth

HSA funds can be invested in stocks, bonds and mutual funds for greater earning potential (fees may apply).

Key features

  • No "use it or lose it"If you don't use it, save it for next year or for retirement. Your balance rolls over indefinitely.
  • Flexible contributionsContributions can come from you, your employer or third parties, up to your federal tax filing deadline.
  • You're in controlChoose when to use your HSA or pay out of pocket. You can fund it from existing HSAs, MSAs or IRAs.
  • Age 65 flexibilityAfter age 65, HSA funds can be used for non-qualified expenses with only income tax due and no penalty.

Benefits glossary

Glossary of Terms

A clear, practical guide to the plan types, costs and accounts you will see during open enrollment.

Plan Types & Network Models

HMO Health Maintenance Organization

A managed care plan where all non-emergency care must come from a defined local network of providers.

Network & referrals
You choose an in-network Primary Care Physician (PCP), and you need a PCP referral to see a specialist.
Out-of-network
Not covered, except for life-threatening emergencies.
Cost
Lowest premiums and lower copays, with the least provider flexibility.

PPO Preferred Provider Organization

A flexible plan with a broad network of preferred doctors and facilities, plus partial coverage for out-of-network care.

Network & referrals
No PCP required. You can see specialists directly without a referral.
Out-of-network
Covered, but with separate, higher deductibles and coinsurance.
Cost
Highest premiums in exchange for the most freedom to choose providers.

EPO Exclusive Provider Organization

Combines the direct specialist access of a PPO with the network limits of an HMO.

Network & referrals
No PCP or specialist referrals needed, but care is covered only at in-network providers.
Out-of-network
Not covered, except emergencies. You pay 100% for non-emergency out-of-network care.
Cost
Moderate premiums, lower than PPOs and similar to HMOs.

POS Point of Service

Combines HMO-style coordinated care with the option to go out of network like a PPO.

Network & referrals
You choose an in-network PCP and get PCP referrals for specialist visits.
Out-of-network
Covered. If your PCP refers you, the plan pays more. If you go on your own, coverage drops and you may need to file the claim yourself.
Cost
Moderate premiums with PCP-coordinated care.

HDHP High-Deductible Health Plan

A plan with a higher deductible and out-of-pocket maximum set by IRS rules, in exchange for lower premiums.

HSA compatible
A qualified HDHP is the only type of health plan you can pair with a Health Savings Account (HSA).
How you pay
Except for preventive care, which is covered at 100%, you pay the full contracted rate for care and prescriptions until you meet the deductible.
Cost
Lowest payroll deductions, with more cost up front when you use care.

Costs & How You Pay

Premium

The fixed amount paid every pay period or month to keep your coverage active, whether or not you use care. With employer plans, the cost is usually split between the employer and your pre-tax payroll deduction.

Deductible

The amount you pay each plan year for covered services before the plan starts paying.

Embedded
On a family plan, each person has their own deductible. Once one person meets it, the plan starts paying for them even if the family total is not met.
Aggregate
The full family deductible must be met before the plan pays coinsurance for anyone on the plan.

Copayment (Copay)

A flat dollar amount you pay at the time of service, for example $25 for primary care or $15 for a generic prescription. Copays are predictable, usually are not subject to the deductible on non-HDHP plans, and count toward your out-of-pocket maximum.

Coinsurance

Your percentage share of covered costs after you meet the deductible. On an 80/20 plan, the plan pays 80% of the allowed amount and you pay 20% until you reach your out-of-pocket maximum.

Out-of-Pocket Maximum

The most you will pay for covered in-network care in a plan year. Deductibles, copays and coinsurance all count toward it. Premiums, out-of-network balance bills and non-covered services do not. Once you reach it, the plan pays 100% of allowed in-network costs for the rest of the year.

Allowed Amount & Balance Billing

Allowed amount
The maximum payment the insurer and an in-network provider have agreed on for a service.
Balance billing
When an out-of-network provider bills you for the difference between their full charge and the allowed amount. The federal No Surprises Act prohibits this for emergency care and other care you could not choose.

Tax-Advantaged Healthcare Accounts

HSA Health Savings Account

Who owns it
You own it. It goes with you if you change jobs or retire.
Who can contribute
Employee and/or employer
Plan requirement
Must be enrolled in a qualified HDHP
Rollover rules
Funds roll over every year. No use-it-or-lose-it rule.

FSA Health Care Flexible Spending Account

Who owns it
Employer-owned. Stays with the company if you leave.
Who can contribute
Employee and/or employer
Plan requirement
Available with any plan, or no plan
Rollover rules
Use it or lose it, subject to a grace period or limited carryover.

Limited-Purpose FSA

Who owns it
Employer-owned. Stays with the company.
Who can contribute
Employee and/or employer
Plan requirement
Paired with an HDHP and HSA
Rollover rules
Limited carryover. Dental and vision expenses only.

Dependent Care FSA

Who owns it
Employer-owned. Stays with the company.
Who can contribute
Employee and/or employer
Plan requirement
Available with any health plan
Rollover rules
Use it or lose it. Covers qualifying child and elder day care.

HRA Health Reimbursement Arrangement

Who owns it
Employer-owned and 100% employer-funded.
Who can contribute
Employer only
Plan requirement
Set by the employer
Rollover rules
Set by the employer's plan document.

HSA Triple Tax Advantage

  • Contributions go in pre-tax or are tax-deductible.
  • Your balance grows tax-free through interest or investments.
  • Withdrawals are tax-free when spent on IRS-qualified medical expenses.
  • At age 65 and older, you can withdraw for non-medical expenses without penalty, paying only ordinary income tax.

Enrollment Rules & Protections

Qualifying Life Event (QLE)

A major life change that lets you change your benefits outside of open enrollment, also called a Special Enrollment Period.

Common events
Marriage, divorce, birth or adoption, death of a covered dependent, or losing other group coverage, such as when a spouse changes jobs.
Timeline
Changes generally must be made within 30 days of the event, or 60 days for loss of Medicaid or CHIP.

Section 125 / Cafeteria Plan

The IRS rule that lets your premiums, FSA and HSA contributions come out of your paycheck before federal income, Social Security and Medicare taxes. Because of this rule, once open enrollment closes your elections are locked in for the full plan year unless you have a qualifying life event.

Prior Authorization & Step Therapy

Prior authorization
Your provider must get the insurer's approval, showing medical necessity, before a procedure, surgery or specialty drug is covered.
Step therapy
You may need to try a lower-cost or generic drug first before the plan covers a higher-cost brand.

Formulary & Drug Tiers

The formulary is your plan's list of covered prescription drugs.

Tier 1
Generic. Lowest cost, with the same active ingredients as the brand name.
Tier 2
Preferred brand. Brand-name drugs on the plan's preferred list.
Tier 3
Non-preferred brand. Higher-cost brands when a generic or preferred option exists.
Tier 4
Specialty. High-cost biologics, injectables and complex therapies, often requiring prior authorization.

Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)

A standard document required by the Affordable Care Act for every plan. It lists covered services, deductibles and copays, with sample cost scenarios such as having a baby, so you can compare plans side by side.

Voluntary & Supplemental Benefits

Short-Term Disability (STD)

Replaces part of your weekly income, typically 50% to 70%, if a non-work illness, injury or recovery from childbirth keeps you from working, after a short waiting period.

Long-Term Disability (LTD)

Replaces part of your monthly income when a disability lasts beyond the short-term period, typically starting after 90 or 180 days, until you recover, reach retirement age or hit the benefit limit.

Critical Illness Insurance

Pays a lump-sum cash benefit directly to you when you are diagnosed with a covered illness such as cancer, heart attack or stroke, separate from your health plan.

Accident Insurance

Pays set cash amounts for accidental injuries and related care, such as ER visits, fractures, dislocations and concussions.

Hospital Indemnity

Pays a fixed cash benefit directly to you for each day you are admitted to the hospital, helping cover high deductibles.

EAP (Employee Assistance Program)

A free, confidential program offering short-term counseling, legal consultations and financial wellness support for you and your household.
